## 1. Owner's rules versus the planner

- **Never cut a usable outro.** Cuts are eligible and cost 12 per bar after one bar of slack. I gave both tracks continuous measured kick. The planner chose `(68, 84, 98)` at cost 13.50 on a 100-bar outgoing track. A natural ending at 100 was available at cost 18.00. The selected plan removes two bars and reports one. The planner has no test of whether an outro is usable.
- **Use original phrasing.** The candidate generator keeps the switch out of the outgoing tail extension and puts any incoming repeat before the switch. But the timing costs use the loop-extended end, so adding a tail loop can change which switch wins before the original end. Outgoing loop content is also modeled by repeating the last measured bar's flags, without choosing or checking the audio span to repeat.
- **Use fills and section boundaries in their musical roles.** Entry prefers a fill end, then a section start, then an unphrased point. Blend 2 itself uses the unphrased fallback. Switches use fill ends and section starts, with a premium for hitting both tracks. The owner accepted one-sided switches, and his words give no fixed premium for a second hit. Fill starts are used only as end targets, and the planner can skip the next eligible fill or section for a later one. It trusts supplied section markers, so it has no way to reject a pads-only change like the PAX example. It also has no preference for the first incoming kick, the outgoing fill into the outro, or the specific long-intro/long-outro placement the owner described. Several of those are examples whose priority he never made universal.
- **Stay in the mix zones.** At 125 BPM the 64-bar search extends about 123 seconds, roughly twice the owner's stated minute. Incoming end targets reach 80 bars, about 154 seconds. The latter allowance has no explanation in the code.
- **Keep bottom end through the blend.** With complete measurements and no overrides, the final mode excludes incoming lows before the switch, then counts incoming kick or bass and outgoing kick. Music cannot rescue a flat bar. That matches the stated intent, assuming the measured outgoing kick still supplies lows after EQ. A `carries` override can count the outgoing side after the switch even with no measured kick, and label fallback can count a `BREAK` with no verified bottom end. Sampling in whole-bar steps also misses fractional changes. Brief gaps are a soft cost per the owner. Making the first eight bars of every gap free is the builder's choice.
- **Incoming level and pictures.** Full kick from bar zero is allowed. The module models the incoming low cut for scoring, with no gain or EQ instructions in the returned plan. The owner's picture requirements for fill times, track ends, bass entry and colour legends are outside these files.

## 4. Measurement

`has_kick` uses two independent per-bar percentiles. Hats or claps can raise the drum envelope while a bass note raises the low band, producing a kick flag even if their peaks never coincide. After the outgoing bass is cut, that false kick can still count. A quiet kick can miss either relative threshold. The P90 bass test helps with the sidechain pattern in the tests, but a narrower or quieter bass pulse can miss it, and deep ducking can defeat the P20 sustain check. A long kick tail can pass that sustain check. The vocal and other stems can also misreport music through leakage or quiet parts, though that changes diagnostics and legacy scoring rather than the final bottom-end cost.

Call 911's half-bar lead-in is present in both JSON files and used by the measurement code. Dropping it shifts windows by about 0.97 seconds. At 125 BPM a 0.2 BPM error accumulates about 0.30 bars over 190 bars. Both errors can shift or smear a fill across the bar whose flag determines the cost. The cached envelopes were absent from the pack, so I checked the supplied flags and their consequences.
