### 1. Implied grid

`implied_points()` steps by 8 from each snapped section start, stops strictly before the next section or track end, and gives every second step the stronger 16-bar grade. A coincident section start or fill end appears once as a marked point. The code also inserts an anchor at bar 0 regardless of the first supplied section.

- A two-bar pickup can shift every subsequent implied point by two bars if its section line is treated as a downbeat. A fill end that genuinely restarts the phrase has the opposite problem: the code marks the fill end but continues the old grid.
- A 12-bar section gets an implied point at +8, four bars before the next section. A 6-bar section gets none. Both results follow the arithmetic. Their musical validity depends on whether the short section resets the phrase or sits inside the existing count.
- A first section at bar 12 generates a phantom implied point at bar 8 in the lead-in. An anchor needs to mean a confirmed phrase reset, including when the file starts before the first musical downbeat.

An implied point can win even two bars from a marked one. In a synthetic pair, implied incoming bar 8 ended naturally and won at `104/112/128`, cost 7.15. The marked bass-in at bar 10 needed a six-bar loop and scored 11.25. That preference can make sense when the natural exit matters. A small clock or loop-cost saving alone should carry less weight than a confirmed musical change. The exclusion of two implied points is a builder choice. Measured bass-in has the inverse problem: a marked outgoing point lined up with a measured bass-in one bar before a section can be classified as one-sided.
