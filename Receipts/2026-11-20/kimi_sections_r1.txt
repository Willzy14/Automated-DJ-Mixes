  2. BAR ONE

  Code check against the claim: the brief and the builder say the shift happens "only when the first sounding beat is a kick". The code (stem_detector.py:210-246) checks the DRUMS STEM: `dp[k] < 0.5 * median(dp)` -> keep grid; any drum hit at half the median level passes, not a kick. A track opening with a snare/clap pickup, a percussion roll, or a snap on the last beat of a bar gets its grid moved to that hit. That is the code being broader than the stated rule; either the rule should say "a drum" or the check should use the kick model output.

  When it moves bar one wrongly:
  - Quiet atmospheric intro: an ambient pad or filtered texture intro sitting at 0.10-0.14 of the body median for 4+ beats, followed by a kick, is classified as silence and skipped. If Sam counts the ambient intro as bar 1 (which DJs routinely do), the whole grid and every line jumps forward. The measured safety margin is a factor of ~1.7 on each side, from 7 tracks.
  - Pickup/anacrusis: a kick pickup one beat before the true bar 1 moves the grid one beat early; every line lands on the .75. Whole-beats-only keeps the phase but not the bar.
  - Reverse cymbal: a swell rising through the nominal silent beats can push a partial-beat peak above 0.15 and stop the skip early, or the skip can stop one beat late; the grid then differs by a beat from the owner's count.
  - Half a beat of silence: correctly not skipped (whole beats only); no issue.
  - First sound not a kick and not a drum (vocal, pad): guarded, grid kept. Good.

  Downstream blast radius when it moves on a track that was right: every section start_sec is recomputed against the new zero point; n_bars changes; hints (first_drop_sec, last_bass_drop_sec, outro_start_sec) change; the mix.md convention documented in the code itself ("stem bar 0 == the track's downbeat == the sections-JSON zero point", stem_detector.py:1117-1118) is broken for shifted tracks (sections[0].start_sec is now e.g. 1.0s, not 0.0). align_engine.load_track and phrase_viz were not run. Old Hints/track_hints.json and any warp-marker/.ls artifacts derived from the old grid are stale by the shift amount, and nothing invalidates them. Note also the scorer's bar1 column exists precisely to catch this, but the headline found count cannot catch a uniform shift under 1 bar (see point 7).

  7. THE SCORER

  score_sections.py can flatter the detector in specific ways:
  - A systematic grid shift of just under 1 bar still scores every line "found": matching is one-to-one (so one detector line cannot match two key lines - that part is sound), but tolerance is 1.0 bar and matching is in bar space after lead-in correction. A detector that is uniformly 0.9 bars early scores 53/53 found with all the error invisible except in the bar1 column, which is printed but not gated. Call 911 on main is the proof: bar1 -0.50 and it still matched 3 of 6.
  - Fills are counted found by ANY overlap in time, even a sliver, and any kick gap (kick_dropout to kick_return) also counts as a fill find, while gaps are excluded from the extras count. That asymmetry means the fill side can only look good: 53/53 with 4 extras is softer than the line score. A fill shifted a whole bar can still overlap a 2-bar key fill and count.
  - Extras for lines are counted correctly (one-to-one, nearest-first; Ninetoes 179.999 and Call 911 64.001 show up). No flattery there.
  - Labels are not scored at all, which is fair because Sam confirmed positions only, but it means "51 of 53" says nothing about intro/drop/break/outro naming.

  Net: the headline numbers are real but the fill number and any bar-shifted line number are generous. The builder's own claims mostly survive; the scorer would not catch a half-bar-everywhere regression, which is exactly the bar-one risk.

  8. MERGE JUDGEMENT

  Before this replaces main's detector, all of the following must be true:
  - Every downstream reader is run against new output on real audio: align_engine (mix builds), phrase_viz, the hints gate with fresh Hints files, and one Ableton render checked against a picture. The builder states none were run.
  - The skipped corpora (Revoloution, Double Dutch, James Poole, and whatever else the 47 skips cover) are re-run and their pinned expectations either pass or are re-justified track by track. These are the only real-audio checks outside the 7 fitted tracks, and they are currently silent.
  - A schema/version field is added to SECTIONS_STEM JSONs and enforced in `_check_cached_sections_inner` / `_may_overwrite_sections_json` / align_engine, with a one-time cache invalidation or migration. Without this, old-grid and new-grid JSONs mix silently.
  - The four holdout pictures are confirmed by Sam (owner check, not score check).
  - The bar-one shift needs owner eyes on a broader set than 7 tracks, specifically tracks with quiet intros and percussion pickups, or it lands behind a flag.

  Should it land behind a flag first: yes. The natural shape is what the codebase already does for soft_intro_outro: a flag (or a sections_schema version plus new cache filename) that lets the pipeline run both detectors, score both on the next real mix, and let Sam pick from pictures. Given "every later step depends on those lines", an unconditional merge with unrun downstream callers and unversioned caches is not safe. The direction is right and the builder's disclosure is unusually good; the blockers are wiring and validation, not the approach.

  Stop-rule check: (i) every rule that moves only one track in the ablation is disclosed in a comment (full-stop -> Back In The Dance; bar-one -> Call 911; bass-in-kick -> PAX), so no hidden single-track fit found; (ii) the bar-one change CAN silently corrupt a previously-right track, and its blast radius (hints, alignments, stale caches) is unaddressed; (iii) cached outputs CAN be mixed across versions, there is no schema field and the provenance gate does not distinguish old from new; (iv) no pinned test was weakened without a stated reason. Two prongs fail -> CORRECTION, not SOUND. The brief's own uncertainties (points 2, 3, 8) are exactly where the failures are.
