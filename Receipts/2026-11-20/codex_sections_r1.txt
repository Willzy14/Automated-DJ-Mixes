## 2. Bar one

`detect()` invokes `_skip_leading_silence` even when the caller supplied the downbeat. The helper looks at the **peak anywhere in a beat window** and uses the drums envelope before the kick model runs. It never establishes that the sound is a kick, that it starts at the window boundary, or that the beat is beat one.

- A beat-four kick or snare pickup after three empty beats can become bar one. I reproduced a 1.5-second move at 120 BPM with that envelope. A quiet vocal, reverse cymbal or anacrusis below the cutoff can be skipped before a drum hit. A louder pickup with little drum energy stops the search, which can leave the real downbeat uncorrected.
- A file with half a beat of silence and then a kick can keep its original anchor because the first window contains that kick. I reproduced no shift in that case. A non-kick first sound can trigger a shift if it hits the drums threshold. With no drums envelope, the helper skips that check entirely.
- Whole-beat moves preserve the beat lattice at fixed BPM but can rotate the bar phase. At 124 BPM, Call 911 moves by 0.9677 seconds, or two beats. The old score's `-0.50` means its reported bar-one anchor was **early** relative to the key. Some later lines can still land late.

The move changes bar buckets, `n_bars`, model sampling, cue and region timestamps, and which audio the first section covers. A warp writer that uses the old origin can place clip beats on the wrong source beats. One that adopts the new origin can renumber bars or exclude an audible pickup. A grid gate that checks only BPM or nearest-beat agreement could pass a two-beat bar-phase error. A gate that expects zero or compares absolute anchors could reject the new result. The pack does not contain those consumers, so their exact behavior is unverified. Cached bar-indexed results, hints and any derived layouts or warp markers must share the same grid identity before they can be reused together.

Keep the caller's authoritative grid fixed. Treat inferred bar one as a separate suggestion until the pipeline explicitly adopts it and verifies the resulting clip start, warp markers and gate behavior.

## 7. Scorer

Section matching is one-to-one. It cannot use a detector line twice, and unmatched duplicates after the first section count as extras. Closest-first is greedy and can **reduce** the number of matches. For key bars `[16, 17]` and detections `[15.2, 16.2]`, it finds one match at the one-bar tolerance although two are possible.

One bar is four beats, too coarse to establish fill-aligned placement. The quarter-bar column also allows a full beat of error. Call 911's break at 106 gets credit for key bar 107. At one-beat tolerance the replay has 49 hits and five unmatched detector lines. The scorer skips the first section, ignores labels and track end, and reads `signals.downbeat_sec` while the writer emits `analysis.downbeat_sec`. Its first-section fallback happens to reproduce the headline in this pack.

Fill scoring gives credit for any positive overlap with an emitted fill **or** a closed kick gap of any length. One broad interval can satisfy several key fills. Kick gaps never count as extras, and duplicate or oversized emitted fills escape an extra whenever they touch a key fill. I reproduced **51/53 lines and 3 extras** and **53/53 fills and 4 extras** with the supplied scorer. Requiring distinct emitted fills reduces fill hits to **47/53** with overlap, or **42/53** with overlap and both endpoints within one beat. Six key fills got credit solely from kick gaps. The main totals are reported in the pack, with no equivalent raw main replay here to rescore.

The scorer also picks the lexicographically first filename sharing a track-name prefix and exits successfully on a partial replay. For a merge comparison, require exact track/version identity and all expected results. Report explicit fills and kick-gap overlap separately, score both fill endpoints one-to-one, and give bar one, clip end and label-dependent planner outcomes their own checks.
