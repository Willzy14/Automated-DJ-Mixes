## 4. Are the acceptance tests able to fail?

**(a) Core builds, report diff shows only T5 changed.** This can fail, and it is the best of the four. It would catch a rule that refuses too much (T1, T3, T10 would change) or that fails to fix T5. **(b) Truth scorer on the three mixes the rule was derived from.** In-sample by construction. Worse, the metrics are loop presence, chunk class and total class. **None of those measure where the loop starts relative to the swap.** A scorer that never looks at the insert beat cannot fail on the defect Rule S exists to prevent. It can only fail on a change in presence or size.

And the predicted numbers in the plan are internally inconsistent with the rule. The replay report says the pipeline still emits Sept T7 (4bx3), Defected T4 (1bx8) and Defected T8 (2bx4) today, and all three have pipeline-side `swap_out_on_loop: true`, so Rule S fires on all three. **(c) Corpus replay with `--policy NAME`.** The tool does not take a policy today (the plan says so itself). The plan's own risk 5 ("a baseline refresh hides an unrelated change") names this and proposes comparing the refreshed pair set against the replay's refused set. And there is a plain contradiction in the plan's own criteria: (d) says `overlap_bars` "must not move", while (a) requires `overlap_bars` to move 52 -> 42 on T5. As written the two acceptance criteria cannot both be satisfied. That is a drafting error that will be found during the build.

using `validate_mix_plan_als.py:322-343`'s own arithmetic (import it; do not re-derive it). Assert it for the Core Sample, for the three corrected mixes, for the corpus replay, and for the two holdouts, under both `interim_v1` and `interim_v1_tailsafe`.

Then the part that makes it able to fail: **three negative controls in the same test file.**
1. A deliberately wrong implementation that refuses *every* outgoing loop must make the "no transition gets worse" assertion fail. If it does not, the scorer is toothless.
2. A deliberately wrong implementation that refuses only loops whose *source* starts before the swap (rather than whose *insert* does) must make the direct `insert_at_beat >= swap_beats` assertion fail. That is the distinction Rule S is actually about, and no presence/size metric can see it.
3. A frozen fixture of the Core T5 geometry (insert 2756, source 644-660, 2 reps + 8 beats partial, swap 2784) must be asserted to FAIL the validator predicate before the rule and PASS after, so the test is known to be sensitive to the defect it exists to prevent.

**Add a second test: the frozen-geometry matrix.** Run the rule through the **real entry points** (not `plan_fill_or_cut` in isolation) on synthetic geometries that cover: primary path, D2 fallback path, legacy path, decisions path, a no-outro track, a non-outro loop source, a fractional outro start, a loop exactly at the swap, a loop after the swap, a pre-swap loop whose repeats land the swap on a later boundary, an incoming intro loop present, and a pair whose final overlap would fall below 16 bars if the loop were removed. For every row assert: (1) the validator predicate passes on the resulting plan; (2) `insert_at_beat >= swap_beats` for every outgoing loop on every path Rule S claims to cover; (3) every pair that builds today still builds (assert the MixPlan overlap window check passes). Row 13 of that matrix is the one I expect to fail the plan as written, and finding that out before the production flip is the whole point of writing it.

## 5. The refusals

**"Sam deletes short stutter loops" does NOT hold. The plan is right.** Verified from the jsonl: Sam **kept** 1-bar and 2-bar loops (Defected T1 1bx7, Core T1 2bx6, August T1 1bx16, August T8 1bx8, August T11 1bx1, Defected T7 2bx3, Defected T10 2bx5, August T4 2bx3+1b) and **deleted** 1-bar and 2-bar loops (Defected T4 1bx8, Defected T8 2bx4, Core T9 2bx4). Chunk length has zero predictive power in either direction. Refusing a length-keyed rule is correct.

**"Pull every overlap toward 32 bars" does NOT hold. The plan is right, and its arithmetic checks out.** Sam's overlaps within 32 +/- 2: Core 6/10 (32, 32, 32, 32, 33, 32), Sept 4/7 (30, 32, 32, 32.1), Defected 5/11 (30, 33.1, 33, 33.1, 32). 15 of 28, range 17.4 to 72.4, so a target would fight 13 of 28. Correctly refused.

**Is there a rule with as much support as Rule S that the plan missed?** The plan's own section 1 table lists several deferrals. Two of them are under-rated:

- **The post-swap tail length.** The V5 write-up's finding 2 is "16 in, 16 out": Sam's post-swap tails are 30, 16, 16, 23.4, 5.7, 32, 16, 16, 17, 16 on Core, a median of 16 with 7 of 10 inside 15-18. The jsonl's `tail_after_swap_bars` corroborates across the other three mixes: Sept 4 of 7 inside 15-18, Defected 5 of 11, August 5 of 10. That is **14 of 28**, the same order of support as the "within 32 +/- 2" count (15 of 28) that the plan treats as a solid rule. The pipeline's post-swap tails, by contrast, range 2 to 48 bars. A rule "the outgoing's tail after the swap should be about 16 bars" is measurable in the same field the plan already reads, and it is the rule that would let the pipeline *produce* Sam's shape rather than merely avoid breaking it. The plan mentions a fragment of it ("Loop starting at the swap that lengthens a short post-swap tail: Defected T2, T7, T11") but files it under "loop shape" and defers it, and it never states the tail-length distribution as a target in its own right.
- **The 12-bar reach cap (R3) is contradicted three times on Defected** (T2 16 bars, T5 20 bars, T11 16 bars). The plan does flag this, in open question 2 and in Found Unasked 6, but leaves it as a "production risk, not created here" rather than as an open contradiction of a shipped rule. It is shipped, it is on by default in `INTERIM_V1` (`max_outgoing_reach_bars=12.0`, `transition_policy.py:142`), and Sam overrides it three times in one mix.

**Is "do not re-target a refused loop to start at the swap" the right call?**

The plan's reasoning here is muddled and should be rewritten. The six "at the swap" loops are the ones Rule S **allows**; they are not evidence about the refused set at all. The real question is: when the rule refuses a pre-swap loop, should the pipeline delete it or move it to the swap? The plan says delete, and says "do not re-target... yet (needs a mid-track insert point)".

That reason is an engineering-cost statement dressed as a principle, and the cost is smaller than stated: the consumer already inserts a clip at a mid-track position (HARTY's loop is inserted at bar 168 of a 196-bar track, and its source is at bars 1-2). What a swap-anchored loop actually needs is (i) source material at or after the swap, and (ii) an insert point exactly at the swap, which changes what gets displaced. That is a genuine design question, not an impossibility.

The evidence leans one way and the plan does not use it: **on the one real case, deleting is right.** Core T5's no-loop fallback gives Jewel Kid 6 bars past the swap; Sam's own version has Jewel Kid 5.7 bars past the swap. And the two pipeline loops Sam deleted on the 22.09 build (T5, T9) were deleted, not moved. So "delete, do not move" is supported. But the plan should say that, with these numbers, instead of "needs a mid-track insert point". It should also note that moving the loop changes the displaced material and the target, which is a real reason to keep it out of a crash fix. (The prior review in the packet reached the same conclusion on this point by a different route; I reached it from the V5 write-up's own numbers, which the plan had not used.)

**One refusal is wrong on the evidence.** Section 4's "Any removal rule keyed on chunk length (contradicted)" is right, but section 4 also says "Changing R3's 12-bar cap" is not being built. Given three direct contradictions on Defected in one mix, that refusal needs at least a *measurement* in piece D (count the corpus pairs where R3 refuses a loop Sam would keep), not a flat no.

## 6. The open questions for Sam

**Question 1 is the right question but is framed as a false binary.** "Should the pipeline play the outro straight through with no loop, or loop starting at the swap?" omits the third option the plan itself relies on elsewhere: move the swap, or re-anchor (Sam did exactly this on Core T3, Core T8 and, on Core T5 itself, moved the swap 2.5 bars later). It also does not mention the option the plan should actually be offering, which is neither of the two: **keep the loop and choose a chunk length or a target that lands the swap on a repeat boundary.**

**Question 2 (the 12-bar cap, genre-scoped or not) is the right second question but is framed backwards.** The Defected examples do not establish a genre boundary; three counterexamples in one mix establish that the cap is wrong sometimes. The productive question is "is 12 a limit on automatic reach, or only on automatic reach without a hand-directed target?", not "tech house or not".

**There is a third question the build cannot proceed without, and it is the one that actually decides Core T5:**

> When the outgoing's tail loop is needed to reach the incoming's next section, but the chunk length available cannot place your bass swap on a repeat boundary, do you want (a) a different chunk length or a different target that lands the swap on a boundary, keeping the outgoing extended, or (b) no loop at all, with the outgoing ending early?

That is the decision the plan is making on Sam's behalf, and the plan's Rule S silently picks (b) for every such pair. The two options are audibly different. The plan cannot claim "the evidence supports this rule" while choosing one of the two branches of this question without asking.

**And a fourth, narrower one that only blocks the completeness claim:** does the rule also apply to hand-authored decisions (`--decisions`) that satisfy the validator? The plan builds a guard in `propose_arrangement` that "applies whatever the policy", which implies yes; the plan never says so.

## 7. The build split

**A and E both edit `align_engine.plan_fill_or_cut`.** The plan says so and sequences E after A merges. That is correct handling, not a defect, but the plan should say explicitly in section 6 that E is not a parallel piece and that A and E must not be in flight at once, since both would touch the same function and the same `skip_outgoing_loop` variable.

**B has a real problem.** "A new `_assert_swaps_clear_of_outgoing_loops` called just before `build_mix_plan` (`:1825`)" cannot work there: at that point the MixPlan does not exist yet, so the guard must re-derive the arrangement geometry and the loop insert beats by hand, duplicating the validator's arithmetic a second time. Two implementations of one rule will drift. It should run **after** `build_mix_plan`, against the MixPlan's own loop contracts, using the validator's own predicate imported from one place. The plan's own line "same arithmetic as `validate_mix_plan_als.py:315-343`" is an instruction to write the rule twice.

**B as specified also breaks the intermediate state.** It "raises in Phase 2 with a hint naming the lane" but "applies whatever the policy". If B merges before the INTERIM_V1 flip (which the plan schedules as a separate, later commit), then the production default `interim_v1` fails B's own guard on every mix containing a pre-swap loop, including the Core build the plan needs to keep reproducible for the before/after comparison, and including the DFTD holdout. B must be gated on the policy flag (or on a "warn only" mode) until the flip. The plan does not say this.

**C cannot be built as instructed.** "Truth file built by script from `pair_history.jsonl` for Sept and Defected" is not executable: `pair_history.jsonl` contains no insert beat, so the script cannot produce the field Rule S is about. The presence/shape part is derivable; the Rule S part is not. C's fixture must be built from Sam's ALS geometry (or the rule must be scored against a field that exists, which is a different and weaker score), and the plan should say which.

**D can be tested alone for plumbing** (the plan says so). It cannot produce the number section 2 depends on ("if piece D shows the rule affects more than 10 percent of looping corpus pairs") until A exists. Section 6 lists D as parallel; it is not parallel for that purpose.

**A and B do not collide** (different files), and B's `getattr` tolerance for the missing `outgoing_loop_refused` field is the right call for decoupling. But `getattr` tolerance is only safe if B's guard does not depend on the field. B's guard works on the frozen plan, so it does not. Good.

**A's own test list has a gap.** "Flag off identical to today" and "field resets between calls" are right, but the reset list should include `outgoing_loop_abandoned`, which `plan_fill_or_cut` **never resets** (only `outgoing_loop_not_needed` is reset, at `:2285`; `outgoing_loop_abandoned` is only ever assigned at `:2663`). Any reused `Alignment` carries a stale abandoned reason, which would corrupt the "three fields mutually exclusive" test the plan asks for in risk 4.

## FOUND UNASKED

- **The plan's own predicted scores in 3(b) contradict its own rule.** Rule S also fires on the pipeline's Sept T7 (4bx3), Defected T4 (1bx8) and Defected T8 (2bx4) loops, which the replay report confirms still exist today, and Sam removed all three, so Sept should improve rather than stay 4/7 and Defected should change rather than stay 4/11. Worse, Defected T1's pipeline 1bx7, which Sam **kept**, has pipeline-side `swap_out_on_loop: true`, so Rule S would remove it and make that transition worse, contradicting "none worse". Either the rule fires on more pairs than the plan thinks or the numbers are invented.
- **`swap_out_on_loop` is not the measurement Rule S needs.** It is true both for a benign loop inserted exactly at the swap (Sept T7's 4bx3: the loop is inserted at the swap, so the swap is on the loop's first boundary) and for the Core T5 defect (swap inside repeat 2). The packet needs an **insert beat per loop** to score this rule at all. Adding `insert_at_beat` (or the outgoing source bar at which the loop is inserted) to the learner's geometry record would make this whole class of question answerable, and would be a small, one-off change.
- **There is a second, independent root cause the plan does not name: the primary search's rounding.** `required_boundary_bars = int(round(locked_swap_gap))` (`:2469`) tests a rounded value against a possibly fractional `outro.start_bar`. A fractional outro start can make the primary search place the swap 0.x bars inside a repeat, with no D2 fallback involved. Rule S happens to mask this in the landmark path, but it is live today and it will outlive Rule S if the rule is ever scoped more narrowly.
- **`al.swap_beats` is computed before the loop is planned.** `compute_aligned_positions` sets `al.swap_beats = prev + al.handoff_bar_out * 4.0 - contraction` at `:2990` and only then calls `plan_fill_or_cut` at `:2996`. The frozen swap beat is therefore a pre-insert position, while the validator compares it against a post-insert loop span. That is the actual mechanism of the bug, and it suggests a second, less invasive fix the plan never considers: **recompute the swap beat after the loops are placed**, rather than forbidding the loop. The plan's own Found Unasked 3 gestures at the symptom ("the paired swap landmark moves from 2784 to 2824") without naming the cause.
- **"Under Rule S the locked swap gap is always 0" is wrong.** Rule S fires exactly when `outro.start_bar < handoff_bar_out`, i.e. when `locked_swap_gap` is **positive**. The invariant it establishes is `locked_swap_gap <= 0`, not `== 0`. As written the sentence is a false claim that a reader will check.
- **`handoff_bar_out == o.n_bars` is reachable** (`_mix_cues` adds the track end as a weight-5 cue, `:1019`), so the plan's reason 3 ("the outgoing still plays past the swap") is false exactly at the boundary, and `outgoing_has_post_swap_content` can flip True -> False when a loop is refused. That is the one mechanism by which the refusal can turn a working transition into a hard error in `apply_automation`, and piece D's proposed fields do not measure it.
- **Piece D's field list is missing three of the plan's own risk items.** The plan asks for "distance from the natural end to the nearest incoming section start for refused pairs" (risk 3) and implies the post-swap runway (risk 2), but the tool's new fields are only `outro_start_bar`, `handoff_bar_out`, `loop_starts_before_swap`, `outgoing_loop_refused`. Add `n_bars`, `handoff_bar_out` distance to the natural end, `outgoing_has_post_swap_content`, and the section-line distance, or the replay cannot answer the plan's own risks.
- **`outgoing_loop_abandoned` is never reset** by `plan_fill_or_cut` (only `outgoing_loop_not_needed` is, at `:2285`). A reused `Alignment` carries a stale reason. Add it to the reset and to the mutual-exclusivity test.
- **The Core write-up shows three in-loop swaps on the 22.09 build, not two.** The V5 doc records T5 `165 (inside loop)`, T7 `148 (in loop)` and T9 `136 (in loop)`, and Sam removed the loop on all three. That is one more piece of support for Rule S than the plan claims, and it is also the clearest example of Sam's **second** response (on T7 he restructured the swap as well as deleting the loop), which the plan's model of Sam (delete the loop, nothing else) does not represent.
- **The Core Sample is not in `pair_history.jsonl` at all** (the replay report's own Found Unasked 2). So "13 of 13 across his three corrected mixes" is 13 groups over 12 transitions in **two** mixes with learner records plus one mix reconstructed by hand from a write-up. The truth fixture for Core cannot be script-built from anything in the packet.
- **The `legacy_v1` alignment policy exists and is reachable** (`align_pair` falls back when neither track has musical landmarks, `:1657-1659`), and `plan_fill_or_cut` on that path has **no** swap check of any kind, including the "does the loop end before the swap" check. The plan should state, with a measurement, whether any corpus pair lands there. If none does, say so and gate the path; if some do, Rule S has to cover it.
- **`plan_fill_or_cut` is also called with no policy at `:3069`** (the visualisation path), which defaults to `INTERIM_V1`. A policy-gated rule would silently not apply there. Harmless today, worth a comment.
- **`SAM_V1` has neither R1 nor R3** (the plan's Found Unasked 9) and would not get the new rule either unless it is added. Any A/B on `sam_v1` therefore measures a different rule set than the one being shipped. The new lane should be added to `SAM_V1` too, or the omission should be stated on the lane's face.
- **The Core Sample's frozen MixPlan and the learner's report disagree about loop presence** (the prior review found this; I confirmed it independently). The frozen plan carries exactly four outgoing loops (T1, T3, T5, T10) and no incoming loops at all, while the learner report for the same mix lists apparent incoming loops at T4 and T9. Any truth table for Core must be built from the frozen contract and the corrected set, not from the learner's mislabelled rows, or the scorer will be scoring the wrong thing.

**Where I differ from the prior review in this packet.** It concluded the evidence establishes 13 loop groups but not 13 starts at or after the swap, and it read `swap_out_on_loop` as unable to distinguish the first boundary from a later repeat. I agree with both. I reach a firmer conclusion on two points than it does: first, the jsonl *does* let you classify the eight `on=false` Sam rows as "after the swap" by reconstructing the natural end, and I did so (Sept T2, Sept T4, Defected T5, T9, T10, August T1, T8, T11 all come out strictly after their swap, consistent with the plan); second, the plan's own (b) predicted scores are not merely weak but arithmetically inconsistent with the plan's own rule, which is a sharper defect than "in-sample". I also reach the conclusion by the opposite route on the fix: the prior review recommended a narrower boundary repair; I agree that the *minimal* correct fix belongs in the D2 fallback's chunk and target selection rather than in a blanket refusal, and I would build Rule S as the fallback guard with that narrower repair underneath it, not instead of it.

**Recommended shape of the correction, in one paragraph.** Keep Rule S, but (1) re-scope its claim: it is the guard for the landmark automatic path, not a global impossibility; (2) add the legacy path and the decisions path to the audit, or gate them, before claiming completeness; (3) fix the primary search's rounding, which is a second root cause; (4) replace "delete the loop" with the narrower repair in the D2 fallback first (choose a chunk length or target that lands the swap on a boundary), and let Rule S be the backstop that fires only when no such chunk exists; (5) re-derive the "13 of 13" from a shipped fixture that includes insert beats, and count August, and separate "at the swap" from "before the swap" using that fixture rather than the `swap_out_on_loop` boolean; (6) fix the (a)/(d) contradiction on `overlap_bars` and re-derive the (b) predictions from the rule rather than asserting them; (7) add the insert-beat-versus-swap property test with its three negative controls; (8) put the "different chunk or no loop" question to Sam as a question, because that is the branch the plan is choosing silently.
